Race styles

Everyone races HYROX and Tryka a little differently.

Some of that shows up in your pace. Some of it shows up in how you spend your effort — where you're strong, where you fade, and how you pace the middle of the race. There's no wrong way to race. But knowing your pattern helps you race smarter, and helps you find a partner who complements it, not just matches your pace.

Why this matters for doubles

You can split the stations. You can't split the runs.

In HYROX and Tryka doubles, you and your partner can divide the stations between you however you like — but every run is shared, and the team only moves as fast as whichever of you is slower that lap. That's most of doubles strategy in one sentence: your partner's strengths can cover your weak stations, but nobody can run a leg for you. Recognising your own pattern, and your partner's, does most of the rest.

The Pacemaker

The pattern

A strong, steady runner. Splits stay close across every leg; station times are ordinary, nothing standout either way.

Best paired with

A station specialist — you take on more of the running volume, they strip sled and carry time off both your legs.

Watch for

Going out too hot early because the pace feels easy. The team's overall time still depends on both of you finishing.

The Anchor

The pattern

Strong, sometimes dominant, on the equipment — sled, ski erg, carries, wall balls. Running is where the time goes back.

Best paired with

A Pacemaker whose run pace you can actually hold. The gap can't be minutes, or the runs undo everything you saved on stations.

Watch for

Choosing a partner on strength alone. The team only moves as fast as your combined running.

The Finisher

The pattern

Strong at bodyweight stations — wall balls, burpees, lunges — but the time goes back at the pulling and machine stations: ski erg, rowing, sled pull.

Best paired with

Someone strong on the machine stations — the mirror image of your gaps, not someone who shares them.

Watch for

Treating "strong at bodyweight" as strong everywhere. The pulling stations are where doubles strategy earns you back the most time.

The Steady Hand

The pattern

No big strengths, no big weaknesses. Solid everywhere, and the splits tend to move together race after race.

Best paired with

Almost anyone — which makes you a great first doubles partner for someone racing HYROX or Tryka for the first time.

Watch for

Becoming the one who absorbs an uneven split by default. Agree the split before the race, not mid-race.

The Fast Starter

The pattern

Quick out of the corral, strong opening kilometres — and the back half of the race is where the time gets given back.

Best paired with

Someone who paces the middle of the race for both of you — happy to hold you back early so you're not paying for it at station six.

Watch for

Letting the first ten minutes decide the race. Bank patience, not just pace.

The Closer

The pattern

Conservative opening, deliberately — and the second half of the race is genuinely faster than the first.

Best paired with

A Fast Starter, if you can agree a shared plan: you cover their fade, they cover your slow start, and the average comes out ahead.

Watch for

Starting so conservatively your partner spends the whole first half convinced something's wrong.

The Transition Grinder

The pattern

Solid runs, solid stations individually — but the time between them quietly adds up across the race.

Best paired with

Someone who's raced doubles before and can keep both of you moving between stations without either of you noticing the gap.

Watch for

The easiest style to fix on this list — practising transitions under fatigue closes more time than almost anything else here.

One more thing splits don't show

What you're racing for.

Two athletes with an identical race shape can want completely different things from the same event — one's chasing a PB, the other's there for the day out with friends. That matters just as much as pace, especially at gym SIM events where the field is a genuine mix of both. It's why HyPair matches on goal as well as pace — not just how you race, but why.

Where this goes next

Today, patterns we recognise. Eventually, patterns we can measure.

Right now, race styles are descriptive — a way to think about your own splits, not a score the app assigns you. As more athletes log full race results in HyPair, we're exploring whether real split data can identify these patterns directly, and whether pairing complementary styles — not just matching pace — produces doubles pairs that actually race better together. If it works, it becomes part of how HyPair matches you. If it doesn't tell us anything pace alone doesn't, we'll say so.
